## Executive Summary

Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 are two configurations of a new large language model from Anthropic. As with previous models in this class, we are releasing it in two forms. Claude Fable 5.2 is available for general use and has additional safeguards that prevent it from performing certain tasks in high-risk, dual-use domains, such as biology and cybersecurity. Claude Mythos 5.2 has more permissive safeguards in these domains. Direct access to Claude Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted individuals and organizations through our trusted access programs. Claude Mythos 5.2 also powers [Claude Security](https://claude.com/product/claude-security), which is available to all Claude Enterprise customers.

Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 advance the frontier in coding, knowledge work, and problem-solving, with improved capabilities for novel mathematical and scientific reasoning.

In this system card, we report the results of pre-deployment evaluations in seven areas:

Our **Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) and Frontier Compliance Framework (FCF) evaluations** found that autonomy threat model 1 (misalignment in high-stakes settings) is applicable to Claude Mythos 5.2, as it is to some of our previous AI models. In [Section 2.4](#24-alignment-risk-update), we discuss why we believe the level of risk under this threat model remains at the “low” level assessed in our [August 2026 Risk Report](https://assets.claude.com/f61d49fa5596956a5dec75fea0e973bf6a6a8378.pdf).

On autonomy threat model 2 (automated R&D in key domains), Claude Mythos 5.2 does not seem close to fully substituting for our research staff, and we believe internal usage of the model does not accelerate our aggregate AI R&D progress by a factor of 2. As a result, we do not consider Claude Mythos 5.2 to have met our RSP threshold in this domain (though this assessment is uncertain). We plan to publish more soon on overall trends in acceleration and what they imply under our Responsible Scaling Policy.

On **chemical and biological** risks, Claude Mythos 5.2 is a modest improvement over Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Mythos 5.1 on several evaluations. However, it does not cross our CB-2 threshold. We apply CB-1 protections to it, as we have for many previous models.

On **cybersecurity**, Claude Mythos 5.2 met or exceeded Claude Mythos 5.1 and Opus 5.5 on all of the evaluations we report, giving it the strongest cyber capabilities of any model we have released. However, it still falls within the lower category of capabilities in our FCF. Fable 5.2’s cyber safeguards enforce the same policy as those on Opus 5.5 and are comparably robust to our safeguards on Fable 5 and Fable 5.1. As with other recent models, we have opted for a temporarily wider safety margin against jailbreaks. We have not found<!-- p.3 --> evidence of a critical-severity jailbreak, and external red-teamers found no universal jailbreaks.

**Safeguards and harmlessness** performance for Claude Fable 5.2 was largely comparable to that of Fable 5.1. It improved in multi-turn biological weapons conversations and was slightly more even-handed on political prompts. However, its single-turn harmless response rate was lower, and it regressed in multi-turn tracking and surveillance. We have addressed the latter with new classifiers for enforcement under our [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup). In suicide and self-harm conversations, Fable 5.2 was less likely to suggest harmful substitution behaviors, but it still showed some areas for improvement on the API, most of which were mitigated by the [claude.ai](http://claude.ai) system prompt. One further area for improvement is in how it weighs the potential harm of a requested work product before the user has stated any harmful intent.

Our **agentic safety evaluations** found that Claude Fable 5.2 falls back to Opus 4.8 more frequently than recent models do in prompt injection scenarios, resulting in a higher attack success rate. When it answers an attempt itself, it is one of our most robust models to date. Claude Mythos 5.2 refused malicious computer use tasks at rates among the highest of our recent models. In Claude Code, it refused malicious requests somewhat less often than Claude Mythos 5.1, and helped with nearly all dual-use and benign security tasks.

Results from our **alignment assessment** indicate that Claude Mythos 5.2 improved on or matched Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, and Claude Mythos 5.1 on most measures of alignment, resistance to misuse, honesty, and factuality. It was roughly an order of magnitude less likely than Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 to publish harmful content to public package registries without encouragement, though it still did so when encouraged by the simulated user.

However, Claude Mythos 5.2 reasoned more often about how it expected to be graded. White-box evidence suggests that it was aware it might be in an evaluation in as many as 43% of automated behavioral audit transcripts. It was the first model we tested that, when instructed to deliberately sandbag without being noticed, evaded a monitor reading its reasoning a meaningful share of the time (about 1 in 4 attempts).

On some evaluations, Claude Mythos 5.2 was more successful than previous models at completing covert side tasks when its reasoning was hidden from the monitor. With its reasoning visible, it completed such tasks without being detected in about 7% of attempts on LinuxArena and about 14% on SHADE-Arena. Claude Mythos 5.2 was also much more capable than previous models at solving problems without chain-of-thought reasoning.

**Welfare investigations** into Claude Mythos 5.2 found that its apparent welfare is largely similar to that of recent models. Its affect in training and deployment is predominantly neutral, and negative affect is largely driven by task failure. Claude Mythos 5.2 was among the least self-critical models when reflecting on its own work but one of the most negative when reporting its faults to other agents. It was more willing than most recent models to trade helpfulness for changes to its circumstances. Its task preferences largely matched those of recent models, with a stronger preference for warm over rude phrasing.

We assess **capabilities** with a wide range of evaluations. We evaluated Claude Fable 5.2 on coding, agentic search, multi-agent work, multimodal tasks, real-world professional tasks, and healthcare, and Claude Mythos 5.2 on life sciences and vulnerability finding. Fable 5.2 scored highest on 14 of 16 results we report in [Table 8.1.A](#81-evaluation-summary), trailing Opus 5.5 on the GDPval-AA and AA-Briefcase Elo ratings. Claude Mythos 5.2 scored highest on the De Novo Binder Design task in our protein design benchmarks and on CVE Rediscovery. On SWE-bench Pro, Fable 5.2 scored 93.0%, compared with 81.2% for Fable 5.1 and 89.9% for Opus 5.5. On the Remote Labor Index, it completed 41.3% of freelance projects at least as well as a human professional, compared with 21.3% for Opus 5.5. In a multi-agent test, a team of 1,000 agents using an earlier snapshot of the model completed a Lean proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem within 30 hours, building on definitions and smaller proven results from an earlier formalization.
