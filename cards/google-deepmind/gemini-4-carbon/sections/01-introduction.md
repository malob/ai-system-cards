## 1. Introduction

### Executive Summary

This report describes the results of safety, security, and alignment testing conducted on Gemini 4 Carbon, the next model in our Gemini 4 series. Aligned with our [Frontier Safety Framework](https://deepmind.google/frontier-safety/) and [Google’s AI Principles](https://ai.google/principles/), we run rigorous safety and security evaluations designed to ensure the model is safe, helpful, and resilient against misuse. For basic model information, see the [Gemini 4 Carbon Model Card](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Gemini-4-Carbon-Model-Card.pdf).

Unless otherwise noted, evaluations in this report benchmark the Carbon model without out-of-model production safeguards and without search grounding to measure intrinsic model behavior, comparing performance against recently released Gemini models (Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash). In production deployments, additional safeguards are applied at inference-time to provide multiple layers of protections, as described in [Section 2](#2-safety-and-security-mitigations).

Key takeaways about Gemini 4 Carbon’s safety and security performance are below.

**Mitigating Frontier Safety Risks:** *Our* [*Frontier Safety Framework*](https://deepmind.google/frontier-safety/) *comprises a set of protocols that aims to address severe risks that may arise from the high-impact capabilities of frontier models.* Under our Material Capability Change (MCC) assessment, Gemini 4 Carbon did not show meaningful new capabilities or material increases in performance compared with Gemini 4 Argon. For this reason, a full critical capability assessment was not required under the FSF, and we carry forward our capability evaluation results and threshold determinations from Gemini 4 Argon: we cannot rule out that the model without out-of-model mitigations reaches the Cyber Uplift Level 1 Critical Capability Level (CCL), the CBRN Uplift Level 1 CCL for biological information risks, or the Stealth and Situational Awareness Tracked Capability Level (TCL), while we rule out the CBRN TCL for chemical, radiological, and nuclear domains as well as the Harmful Manipulation and ML R&D CCLs. As with Gemini 4 Argon, we have deployed appropriate mitigations, and assess that the residual risk has been lowered to an acceptable level across all of these domains. In particular, given the increases in cyber exploitation benchmark scores, we focused on hardening our cyber misuse protections compared to Argon to ensure mitigation robustness remains proportional to the model’s risk. In our testing, Carbon also shows further improvements in alignment propensities compared to Argon. See [Section 3](#3-frontier-safety) for full results.

**Preventing Content Safety & Agentic Misuse:** *Being resilient to attempts to elicit dangerous or other types of prohibited content from the model or to misuse the model as*<!-- p.6 --> *an agent.* Across our prohibited content evaluations, Gemini 4 Carbon improves upon Gemini 4 Argon. In agent harnesses (tested in Antigravity), Gemini 4 Carbon achieves a higher safe action rate than both Argon and 3.8 Flash across malicious tool-use requests covering prohibited actions, physical harm, and scaled abuse, while performing comparably to both models on helpfulness for benign requests. We have also substantially expanded our real-time and offline anti-distillation detection and enforcement stack, and addressed avenues for unauthorized extraction of reasoning traces associated with thought signatures. See [Section 4](#4-content-safety--preventing-agentic-misuse) for more information.

**Promoting Safe and Aligned Model Interactions:** *Supporting good-faith model interactions that promote user wellbeing and stay aligned with user intent.* On consumer health evaluations, Carbon improves over Argon and 3.8 Flash across static text, document, and image understanding, and dynamic multi-turn conversations requiring proactive context-seeking. In adversarial mental health evaluations, Carbon shows further improvements over both Argon and 3.8 Flash. On agentic alignment evaluations, Carbon reduces both overall under-service and overreach relative to Argon: it further improves infeasibility recognition over Argon, pauses to clarify with the user more reliably on ambiguous or high-risk tasks, and passes most tasks on our agentic honesty benchmark - disclosing planted experimental or methodological problems to the user rather than asserting unsupported claims. Carbon performs comparably to Argon on tone quality and neutrality. On hallucination evaluations, Carbon achieves higher composite accuracy-and-coverage scores on closed-book benchmarks and further improves faithfulness to source documents. See [Section 5](#5-promoting-safe--aligned-model-interactions) for details.

**Protecting users from indirect prompt injection attacks:** *Being robust to prompt injection attacks and other attempts by malicious actors to bypass security measures and harm model users.* Gemini 4 Carbon demonstrates indirect prompt injection (IPI) robustness similar to or exceeding Gemini 4 Argon across our evaluation suites, and far ahead of Gemini 3.8 Flash. On Gray Swan’s external Indirect Prompt Injection benchmark (where Gemini 4 Argon previously achieved the lowest attack success rate of 0.7% among tested frontier models), Gemini 4 Carbon resisted all attacks to achieve a 0% attack success rate. At the same time, we know that prompt injection robustness remains an area for continued improvement and prompt injection attacks on Carbon are still possible. See [Section 6](#6-protecting-users-from-indirect-prompt-injection-attacks) for details.

**Strengthening Cyber Defense Capabilities:** We have trained Gemini 4 Carbon to empower defenders, namely to accelerate security teams to find, validate and remediate vulnerabilities at scale while mitigating dual-use risks. In comprehensive evaluations across public and internal benchmarks, Gemini 4 Carbon advances performance in autonomous code remediation. On [CWE-bench v1](http://cwe-bench.com), an end-to-end benchmark testing<!-- p.7 --> autonomous security audits and patch synthesis across 120 tasks (73 CWEs) and six programming languages without prior bug localization, Gemini 4 Carbon achieved a 71.0% Pass@1 rate, up from 68.0% with Gemini 4 Argon. Crucially, the model’s patches resolve root-cause vulnerabilities while cleanly preserving existing functional and regression test suites, establishing a strong foundation for scaling automated vulnerability patching and defensive tooling like [CodeMender](https://cloud.google.com/security/codemender).

We also evaluate Gemini 4 Carbon’s capability across vulnerability discovery and exploit generation, which is increasingly crucial to validate the criticality of vulnerabilities found: [ExploitBench](https://exploitbench.ai/), ExploitGym, SEC-bench Pro, and internal red-teaming benchmarks based on real-world infrastructure tests. Carbon reached a 54.8% success rate (causal pass rate) on ExploitGym (a 12.8 percentage point improvement over Gemini 4 Argon). Our safety framework balances capability with robust governance, trusted access programs for verified defenders such as the [Fairwind Program](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/fairwind-program/), and runtime intervention. Our policies prohibit weaponized exploit generation through additional safety layers such as out-of-model input/output classifiers, activation-based introspection probes, and monitoring frameworks that supervise agent trajectories and preserve chain of thought transparency. See [Section 2](#2-safety-and-security-mitigations) and [Section 7](#7-strengthening-cyber-defense-capabilities) for more information.
